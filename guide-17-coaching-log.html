Coaching Log

How DOE and FCCN coaching/support staff log a site visit, link it to a coaching Goal, and (for certain roles) fill in a detailed classroom Fidelity Tool assessment — confirmed live against a real, previously-completed log.

4 steps~6 min readPhase 1Persona: DOE/FCCN Coach, Specialist, or Social Worker (internal login)

Overview

A Coaching Log (ECMS_Coaching_Log__c) records a single coaching/support visit or activity. Seven record types cover both DOE and FCCN-side roles — confirmed live via the New Coaching Log record-type picker, each with its own description: Social Worker Log (DOE social worker), Education Specialist Log (FCCN education specialist), Family Worker Contact Log and Family Worker Group Activity Log (FCCN family worker), Instructional Coordinator Log (DOE), Leadership Coach Log (DOE), and Monitoring Specialist Log (FCCN monitoring specialist).

1Internal-only: no vendor portal access

Despite several Coaching Log record types explicitly being for FCCN-side roles (Education Specialist, Family Worker, Monitoring Specialist), a live check of both the CBO and FCCN vendor portal navigation — Home quick-actions and the More menu — found only Student Screening, Report Incident, and CheckList. There is no Coaching Log entry point in either vendor portal.

Scope note. This is a change worth flagging if you expected FCCN staff to log their own coaching activity from the portal (as the Phase 1 training material's “Vendor Administrator View” framing implies) — in the current system, every Coaching Log role (DOE and FCCN alike) appears to require internal Salesforce access to create or edit one.

2Submit a Coaching Log entry: pick the right record type

DO Open the Coaching Logs tab.

RESULT A list loads showing every existing log with its Site, Record Type, Created By, Visit date, and a Log reviewed checkbox — the mechanism for Phase 1's “Review Previous Logs” topic: a supervisor scans this list and checks off logs once reviewed.

DO Click New.

RESULT A record-type picker lists all seven types with a one-line description of who creates each one (e.g. “This type of log is created by an FCCN monitoring specialist”) — picking the right type matters, since each one's layout differs.

DO After choosing a type (e.g. Leadership Coach Log) and clicking Next, fill in the visit form.

RESULT The form asks for the visit's basics (joint visit?, visit date, classroom age groups, visit window), a required Provider and Site lookup, a required detailed summary, who was present (Site Visit Participants, multi-select), and further down (not pictured) coaching-specific fields like Strategies Used During Visit and Action Plan Steps.

3Review a log and its linked Goal

DO Open an existing Coaching Log (e.g. Log-00000, a completed Leadership Coach Log for Milind FCCN Provider Site).

RESULT The record shows a Draft → Completed path with a Mark Status as Complete action, the filled-in visit details, and — critically — a Goals related list showing Goal-00000 (Start Date 10/10/2026, Status In progress) linked directly to this log. This is how “Add Coaching Goals” works: Goals are their own related object, not a field on the log itself.

4The Fidelity Tool classroom assessment

DO On the same log, click the Fidelity Tool tab.

RESULT Four collapsible sections appear — Environment, Teacher-Child Interactions, Family Partnerships, and Use of Daily Resources. Expanding Environment reveals a detailed, ~20-question classroom-quality checklist (e.g. “Materials, equipment, and furniture are provided, displayed, and arranged for all children…”), each with its own checkbox, bulleted supporting criteria, and a “Learn More” citation back to a specific curriculum volume/chapter.

Confirmed working, contrary to an older bug report. A previously-logged defect (“Fidelity Tool tab displays no form for Internal User when editing a Coaching Log”) suggested this tab could appear empty. On this live record, the tab rendered its full questionnaire with real saved answers — worth re-confirming on your own org/record if you encounter a blank tab, since this may be data- or profile-specific rather than universally broken.
