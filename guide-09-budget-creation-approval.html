Budget Creation & OA → OD Approval

How a CBO's Budget Creator builds a site's yearly spending plan in the vendor portal and submits it, and how that budget then moves through DOE's two-stage internal review — an Operations Analyst (OA) first, then an Operations Director (OD) for final sign-off.

10 steps~13 min read Persona: CBO Budget Creator / Operations Analyst / Operations Director

Overview

Each site has a budget for the fiscal year — a spending plan showing what the CBO/FCCN expects to spend on staff, supplies, rent, and other costs for that site and contract. A Budget Creator at the CBO builds this in the vendor portal and submits it; internally it's stored as an ECMS Budget record, tied back to the site's Contract and Contract Period. From there, the budget goes through DOE approval: an Operations Analyst (OA) reviews it first, then it moves to an Operations Director (OD) for final sign-off — this is the "two-stage" or "OA → OD" approval chain.

Where this fits. Budget approval sits right before invoicing in the ECMS chain: Enrollment → Attendance → Budget approval → Invoice → Payment. Per the ECMS onboarding notes: "Only after a budget is approved can the vendor submit monthly invoices against it." Invoices then go through this same OA → OD review chain a second time.

Steps

1Open the vendor portal home page

DO Log in to the vendor portal as the CBO's Budget Creator.

RESULT The portal home page loads with quick-action tiles for the functions this user was provisioned with, including Create Budget.

2Go to the Budgets list

DO Click the Create Budget tile from the portal home page.

RESULT The Budgets list view opens, showing any existing budgets — here, Cedar Point's own B-00000057, already Approved by DOE from a prior fiscal year — plus a New button to start another one.

3Start a new budget and pick the site

DO Click New. In the "New Budget" dialog, select the site to create a budget for from the Active Sites list — each row already shows the site's contract type and fiscal year, pulled from the underlying contract and contract period — then click Next.

RESULT A wizard opens listing every active site this vendor has a contract for. Sites with a permanent closure are broken out separately and can't be budgeted for. Contact DOE administration if an expected site or contract is missing.

Note: A budget can only be created for a site/contract/fiscal-year combination that already exists as a contract period — the same rule that limits budgets and invoices to seat types the underlying contract actually covers.

4Fill in the budget's spending plan

DO Continue through the wizard, entering the site's planned spending — staff wages and fringe benefits, OTPS (other-than-personal-service costs like supplies and rent), and any budget classes/line items for the classrooms this budget covers. Total Budgeted Amount is calculated from these figures against the site's Total Funding for the fiscal year.

RESULT As figures are entered, the wizard totals them against the contract's funding ceiling for that fiscal year (Total Budget Limit) so the Budget Creator can see whether the plan is within the funded amount before submitting.

Note: This step is a live vendor-portal wizard against a real contract, so it isn't saved or submitted here to avoid creating a duplicate budget record. The saved Funding Information section shown later in Step 10 (Total Funding, Total Budgeted, Total OTPS, Staff Wages and Fringe Benefits) reflects exactly what this step produces once completed.

5Submit the budget for DOE approval

DO Once the spending plan is complete, the Budget Creator clicks Submit (or saves as a draft first and submits later). Submitting locks the budget as an "Approval Request Submitted" event and routes it to the DOE Operations Analyst queue. This status appears when the budget is submitted with a valid FAMIS contract ID.

RESULT The budget's Status changes from "Saved Budget" to "Submitted for Approval", and its Approval History logs a "Submitted" step with the submitter's name and timestamp — this is the same event visible later in Steps 6–8.

Two review chains, same shape. A budget's OA → OD chain is identical in structure to an invoice's approval chain — only invoices can't start until a budget clears this chain first.

6Operations Analyst (OA) review

DO Internally, an Operations Analyst finds their queue of waiting budgets — the Budgets list view includes role-scoped views like Operations Analysts, Operations Directors, and Central Approvers alongside status-based ones like Pre-Approved Budgets, Saved Budgets, and Submitted for Pre-Approval Budgets — then opens a submitted budget, reviews the spending plan against the contract's funding, and clicks Approve or Reject directly on the Approval History tab (or requests more information, which moves Status to "Information Requested").

RESULT Before the OA acts, the Approval History tab already shows the pending step waiting on the Operations Analysts queue, with Approve and Reject buttons ready to use. Once approved, that step is recorded with the OA's name and timestamp, and the chain automatically advances to the OD step.

Once Cedar Point's own B-00000057 went through this same step, the OA step was recorded on its Approval History tab too, showing Oscar Adams as the OA who approved it, followed immediately by the OD step.

7Check progress mid-review

DO Since Cedar Point's own budget is already fully approved, use a different budget still in progress to see what a mid-review record looks like: B-00000054 (Brookside Early Learning Center) is currently sitting between OA and OD review.

RESULT This budget's Status itself now reads "Approved by OA" — a dedicated status for this in-between state, not just a progress-bar marker — and its progress bar shows "Approved by OA" reached but OD approval not yet complete. The OA step is done, and the record is now waiting on the Operations Director.

8Budgets without a FAMIS Contract ID (pre-approval)

DO A budget can be submitted even before its contract has a FAMIS Contract ID — the field DOE's finance system uses to track the contract once it's fully executed. Open a contract that's still missing this field.

RESULT Everything else on the contract is filled in — type, start/end dates, amount — but FAMIS Contract ID is blank. Any budget submitted against this contract automatically routes into a separate pre-approval track instead of the normal one.

Submitting a budget against this contract moves its Status straight to "Submitted for Pre-Approval" instead of the usual "Submitted for Approval."

From here, the budget goes through the same OA → OD review chain as any other budget — just under pre-approval-specific status names at each stage. Once OA approves, Status becomes "Pre-Approved by OA" (instead of "Approved by OA"):

The contract ID is what finishes the job. Once an authorized OA or OD user adds a valid FAMIS Contract ID to the contract, the budget automatically completes — no re-submission or re-review needed. Continuing the same example: once this contract's FAMIS Contract ID was filled in, Budget B-00000088 jumped straight from "Pre-Approved by OA" to "Approved by OA" on its own — no one touched the budget itself. The same automatic completion applies at the fully pre-approved stage: a budget sitting at "Pre-Approved" (both OA and OD done) becomes "Approved by DOE" the moment its contract ID is added, and becomes immediately usable for invoicing. Vendors can't edit the contract ID themselves while a budget is in pre-approval — only an OA/OD user can.

And separately, once OD also approves a pre-approval budget — with the contract still missing its FAMIS Contract ID — Status becomes "Pre-Approved" (instead of "Approved by DOE"):

The same automatic completion shown above for the OA-level transition happens again here, at the fully pre-approved level: adding B-00000076's contract's FAMIS Contract ID moves it straight from "Pre-Approved" to "Approved by DOE" — with the exact same "Invoices may now be submitted for this budget" guidance text and a live Create Invoice button, on its own, with no separate action taken on the budget itself.

Note: This pre-approval path exists so a CBO/FCCN isn't blocked from starting its budget paperwork just because DOE's contract execution is still in progress.

9Operations Director (OD) final sign-off

DO On that same in-progress budget, open the Approval History tab to see the chain so far, including the pending OD step waiting to be actioned by an Operations Director (with Approve/Reject buttons available to whoever is assigned).

RESULT The history shows OA Approval already Approved by Oscar Adams, and the OD Approval step — one still routed to the "Operations Directors" queue, one directly assigned to Olivia Dean and showing Pending. Once an OD approves, Status advances to "Approved by DOE" (as seen on Cedar Point's B-00000057 in Step 6), and the budget becomes usable for invoicing.

10Confirm the approved budget

DO Once every required stage has approved, revisit the budget's detail page and its Approval History tab to confirm its final state.

RESULT Status reads "Approved by DOE," the approval-stage progress bar is complete, and a Create Invoice action button is now available directly on the record — no one has to manually tell the vendor they can begin invoicing; the vendor sees this same "Invoices may now be submitted for this budget" guidance and enabled button the moment they open the budget themselves. Cedar Point's B-00000057 shows exactly this state, with Total Budgeted Amount $500.00 against $1,500.00 of Total Funding, and one invoice (INV-00021) already created against it. Its Approval History tab shows the complete chain: submission, OA Approval, and OD Approval, each with the approver's name and timestamp.

Hard precondition on invoicing. Only after a budget is approved can the vendor submit monthly invoices against it, requesting actual payment based on that month's certified attendance. Invoices then go through this same OA → OD review chain again — see the Invoices guide for that workflow, and the Attendance guide for the monthly certification invoicing also depends on.
